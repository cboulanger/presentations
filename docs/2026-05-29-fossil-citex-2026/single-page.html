# Digging Up Citations: FOSSIL, a Dataset and Workflow for Reference Extraction in Law and the Humanities Christian Boulanger (mpilhlt) Luca Foppiano (ScienciaLAB) <span class=conference-label>CiteX 2026 (May 29 2026, DIPF, Frankfurt, Main) </span> Note: - Work in Progress - Luca could not join - concentrate on workflow and dataset design, work on dataset and performance improvements ongoing --- ### Problem - Existing citation extraction models assume structured bibliographies <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Law/humanities cite mainly in footnotes <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - References mixed with commentary and cross-references <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - High heterogeneity defeats rule or pattern-based parsing <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### The promise and challenges of LLMs - LLMs can handle heterogeneous footnotes, but... <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Unclear reliability: stochastic behavior, hallucinations <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Closed models: third-party dependency, weak reproducibility <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Open-weight models: hardware + cost; still slow <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Key bottleneck: few gold-standard SSH/law datasets <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Existing SSH-oriented datasets | Name | Content | Limitations | | --- | --- | --- | | **CEX** | 112 English articles | Single-language, no footnotes | | **EXparser Gold Standard** | 100 German social-science documents | Single-language, few footnotes | | **LinkedBooks** | 40,000 refs (Venetian history) | Narrow domain | General limitation: limited footnote focus; no shared standard format --- <!-- I would remove this slide --> ### New strategy: document routing - Zhu, Colavizza & Romanello (2026): open-weight LLMs vs Grobid <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Fine-tuned LLMs handle distributin shift better <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Biggest gains: non-English + footnote-heavy documents <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Proposed: route documents between Grobid and adapted LLMs <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Our alternative: strengthen Grobid via targeted training <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Challenges of law and SSH citation practices | Field | Where references live | Role of footnotes | | --- | --- | --- | | Natural sciences, most social sciences | Separate bibliography | Commentary only; often discouraged | | Law, humanities, older social sciences | Footnotes and/or bibliography | Commentary + context + references + cross-refs + locators | --- ![](./uploads/16834afd-ac38-4410-bd15-e32375c01677.png) --- ### Footnote content types 1. Commentary only (no reference) -> discarded <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 2. Biographical notes (often first page) -> discarded <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 3. One or more cleanly separable references <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 4. Cross-references (e.g., “Ibid.”, “See Miller, n. 9, p. 5”) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 5. Mixed content (any order), including signal words indicating citation intent <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Contributions 1. **PDF-TEI Editor**: production-ready web annotation tool <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 2. Documented **annotation workflow** (Editor + Grobid) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 3. **FOSSIL**: open, multilingual dataset (96 articles, >7,600 footnote references) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 4. **Grobid specialised flavor** use case for law/humanities <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### PDF-TEI Editor - Open-source, collaborative TEI-XML annotation <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Synchronized PDF/XML view + TEI schema validation <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Role-based access control <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Pluggable extraction backends (Grobid, LLaMore) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Versioning: branching, merging, change history with attribution <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- <img src=./uploads/9921051d-69f6-4491-9d7a-5fc3464ca502.png class="resize" /> --- ### General workflow - Sources: openly licensed journals (DOAJ, Crossref) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Excluded: non-derivative OA licenses <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - 10 batches, annotated in 5 phases <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Annotation workflow 1. Pre-annotate with latest Grobid model <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 2. Correct (annotator 1) + verify (annotator 2) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 3. Validate by senior reviewer <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 4. Update living annotation guide (tracked via GitHub issues) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> 5. Train + evaluate updated model <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> Note: 7 annotators total (2 senior authors, 4 DH students at University of Stuttgart, 1 reviewer). --- ### Inter-annotator agreement - 194 pairwise comparisons (96 documents) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Krippendorff’s α: mean **0.917**, median **0.989** <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - High reliability; clear learning curve across batches <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### The dataset - FOSSIL (Footnote-based Open-access SSH Scientific Instance Labels) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Open corpus across disciplines, languages, and styles <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Designed for footnote-heavy law/humanities conventions <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Use cases: training / evaluation <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Key facts | | | | --- | --- | | **Selection** | 96 articles from 38 journals | | **Domains** | Law, humanities, history, social sciences | | **Publication dates** | 1959–2025 (mostly 2009+) | | **Languages** | English (84%), German (5%), Portuguese, Italian, French | | **Footnotes** | 2,400 blocks (~500 discursive, ~1,900 bibliographic) | | **References** | >7,600 references (~100 per document) | | **Annotation format** | TEI subset, 3 layers: article / footnote / reference segmentation | --- ### Grobid specialization - Specialized processing “flavor” to replace models incrementally <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Maximizes reuse of the existing Grobid models <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Enables component-level evaluation <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Work in progress --- ![](./uploads/f7ac5691-4063-4107-bdff-5cd348c2ddd8.png) --- ### End-to-end evaluation - 4 law/humanities articles (same set used in [prior LLM-vs-Grobid study](https://cboulanger.github.io/presentations/2025-04-03-extracting-citation-data-from-law-humanities/single-page.html)) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Compare: default Grobid vs **footnotes-ref** flavor <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Gold standard: manually annotated references <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Metrics: token-level F1 - Footnote matching 0.19 -> 0.91 :green_apple: <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - References (within footnotes) matching 0.11 -> 0.59 :yellow_heart: <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Citation fields (see next) :red_circle: <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ![](./uploads/1c72055b-b1e8-4b2f-9e67-412fbdb63b4e.png) Per-field F1: journal title, author surname, publication date, using fuzzy matching (Levenshtein ≥ 0.8) and Hungarian matching for reference alignment (LLaMore) --- ### Citation field extraction - The citation-parser still using the standard Grobid model <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Specialized flavor boosts recall: - author surnames: 0.23 → 0.64 - publication dates: 0.21 → 0.72 <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Non-dates numeric values: volume, issue, pages mixed with cross-reference numbers <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ### Results - Domain specialization markedly improves footnote citation extraction <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Citation and reference segmentation to be completed, hindering field-base results <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Dataset + workflow are needed resources in SSH <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Model specialization: seamless integration in Grobid as baseline <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ## Conclusion - **FOSSIL**: first openly licensed resource of this scale for legal/SSH footnote references <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - End-to-end results: large gains, but substantial headroom <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Hard cases remain: cross-references and mixed-content footnotes <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- ## Future work - Extend FOSSIL: more languages, legal traditions, historical periods <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Cross-reference resolution (still largely open) <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> - Explore small fine-tuning adapter and end-to-end neural pipelines across: - article segmentation - reference segmentation - citation parsing <!-- .element: class="fragment" --> --- **Thank you, let's collaborate!** ![](./uploads/3dbefe18-899c-452d-904d-d7dd8035fa4d.png) We thank Dr. Anselm Küsters (Stuttgart
